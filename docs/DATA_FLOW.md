# What data leaves the PC (for a security review)

Sales Copilot is a Windows desktop app Keith runs during Zoom sales calls. It captures call audio on the PC, turns it into text, and on request suggests his next line.

**While a call is running (between Start and Stop/Pause):**
- **Deepgram (speech-to-text):** both audio streams stream live over an encrypted websocket (`wss://api.deepgram.com`, model nova-3): Keith's microphone, and the meeting audio, meaning everything that plays on the selected headset output (all remote participants, plus any other app's sound on that device). The request sets Deepgram's `mip_opt_out=true` (opt out of Deepgram's model improvement program). Deepgram's retention of audio and text is per Deepgram's terms, for Security to confirm.
- **Anthropic (Claude API):** text, not audio. Each request contains:
  - the last 30 seconds of the transcript word for word (at least the last 3 lines, however old),
  - up to about 1,800 characters of the 3 minutes before that,
  - up to 3 related lines from earlier in the call,
  - words still being transcribed,
  - the call setup Keith typed (call type, goal, outcomes, account name, deployment, and up to 3 things he must learn on the call) and any speaker labels he set,
  - up to 3 sections from knowledge files he approved in the app, and the titles of approved files that are out of date or cover the other deployment,
  - the running call notes (below), up to about 800 characters, led by the must-learns still open or only partly answered (on top of those 800 characters; they are sent even before the first notes, or with notes off).
  - when the call's account matches earlier calls with the same account name, up to about 700 characters from the last 3 of them (`<earlier_calls>`): dated items of what Keith promised, what they said they would do, the agreed next step, questions and proposals still open (from each call's wrap-up, minus items Keith removed), what Keith still wanted to learn when those calls ended (minus ones he removed, and ones he set out to learn again on this call), and from those calls' notes what they wanted and facts they stated about their tools, team, timeline and decision process. Worked out once at call start (again if the account is changed during the call); call notes requests don't include it.
  - for an Another angle press (HELP again soon after a card, with nothing new said), the move and line of the card he already had for that moment, and of up to 2 cards before it that he also pressed past.
  - for a Must learn press (Keith clicked an open must-learn on the plan line during a live call), that must-learn's words, asking for a line that gets there; they are stored with that request (and in a practice moment saved from it).

  A request is sent when Keith presses HELP or WRAP (the button or Ctrl+Alt+W), **and in the background after the other side finishes speaking** (at most 4 a minute, so a suggestion is ready instantly). A WRAP press, or a HELP press when the last 30 seconds sound like the call is ending, sends the same content plus an instruction to help lock the next step. A small keep-alive every few minutes and a key check at app start and call start contain no call content.

  **Call notes** (Setup, step 3, on by default): after about a minute of the other side talking, at most once every 3 minutes (20 times an hour), a background request sends the previous notes, the transcript lines said since them and the call setup (including the must-learns), and gets back the updated notes (short lists of what was said: topic, what they want, open questions, concerns, facts they stated, next steps, and how far each must-learn got: open, partial or done, citing the lines).
- **Right after Stop** (Anthropic, text only, the same model as HELP; they finish even if the next call starts meanwhile, and each covers only the call that ended):
  - **Closing notes update** (when call notes are on): up to 3 requests of the same kind as above, covering the lines said since the last update (the last minutes of the call), so the final notes are complete.
  - **Wrap-up** (Setup, step 3, "Wrap-up after each call", on by default; works with call notes off; none for a call where nothing was transcribed): one request (and one more each time Keith clicks Try again after a failed build) with the call setup (including the account name and the must-learns), the final call notes, speaker labels, and the call's transcript (for a long call, its last 60,000 characters or so; the notes cover the rest). It gets back short lists: what Keith or his team said they'd do, what the other side said they'd do, the agreed next steps, steps only proposed, and their questions not answered on the call.
  - **Follow-up email draft**, only when Keith clicks "Draft follow-up email": one request with the wrap-up items he kept (each with its short quote from the transcript and who said it, by the names in his speaker labels), the call setup, the buyer names from his speaker labels, up to 3 "what they want" from the notes, and up to 5 sections from knowledge files he approved (current, and for this call's deployment only), found by searching his open questions and promises. The draft comes back to the app; it is never sent anywhere: Keith copies it into his own email.
- **Nothing is sent while paused, and nothing after the requests right after Stop above** (a wrap-up Try again and the follow-up draft are sent whenever Keith clicks them), except the optional speed test (Diagnostics), which sends the app's built-in practice scenarios and the playbook, plus, when "Include my saved moments" is ticked (the default once Keith has saved any), the practice moments he saved from his calls: each one is the same kind of request as the live HELP press it came from (the transcript up to that press, the call setup including the account name and the must-learns, speaker labels, the approved knowledge sections that request used, the earlier-calls items that press showed, and for an Another angle press the lines of the cards it was asked on). Locking the PC or putting it to sleep pauses a live call automatically. If nothing is heard from the other side for 10 minutes, the app asks "Still on a call?" and stops a minute later if nobody answers.

**Stored on the PC** (in the app's data folder):
- Call transcripts (text), speaker labels, the call setup for each call (including the account name and the must-learns), the HELP requests and suggestions Keith actually saw (with each card he saw, the other side's words it answered, up to about 90 characters, with the speaker and how many seconds before the press; also in practice moments saved from it), and his feedback (ratings, which lines he used, short notes, and a mark when he pressed again for another angle), in a local database and per-call transcript files. Background requests he never saw keep only timings, cost, which sources were used, the call setup and speaker labels in use then (the same account and names the call already stores), what kind of check failed, and the kind of press it was built for (opening, next step) with, for a possible buying signal, its kind (pilot, rollout, pricing, something for their boss): labels, never words. Not the transcript or card text (older databases are cleaned when the app opens). Every request records the call setup and speaker labels it was built with, and the earlier-calls items it showed (when there were any), so a practice moment saved from it replays the same way even if they were edited later in the call. Those earlier-calls items are copies, kept only on requests Keith saw: they stay with this call's requests if the earlier call is deleted, and go when this call is deleted. Deleting saved calls during a call takes them out of HELP's later requests at once.
- The latest call notes for each call and how many updates ran and what they cost, in the local database; deleting the call deletes them. Logs and scorecards keep only those counts.
- "Last time with <account>" on the call screen is read from the saved calls themselves (their setup, wrap-up and notes); nothing extra is stored, and deleting a call removes it from there.
- The wrap-up of each call (the items, the short quote from the transcript each one came from, Keith's ticks and edits, and the must-learns the call notes still had open or partly answered at the end, shown as "Still to learn" and, unless Keith removes them there, in the next call's "Last time") and the latest follow-up email draft, in the local database; deleting the call deletes them. Logs and scorecards keep only counts (items per section, how many he confirmed, removed, added or edited), timings and cost.
- Copies of the knowledge files Keith added, and their text indexed for search.
- API keys, encrypted with Windows DPAPI (tied to Keith's Windows account).
- Logs and diagnostics: timings, counts, error codes, per-call numbers on how sure speech-to-text was (word count, lowest, median, share unsure), the kind of press and of a possible buying signal (labels such as "pricing" and its call time, never words), device names, short fingerprints of knowledge file names, and some local file paths (which include the Windows user name). Never transcript or card text: automated tests check the call's diagnostics log and the HELP log, including words still being transcribed.
- Per-call HELP scorecards and speed-test reports: numbers only (counts, timings, cost, feedback taps); speed-test reports also hold the model's answers to the built-in practice scenarios. A speed-test report from a run that included Keith's saved moments (in `reports/mine`, file name ending `-mine`) also holds those moments' titles (account and date) and the model's answers to them.
- Practice moments Keith saves after a call ("Save as practice moment", in the `practice` folder): call text up to that HELP press, speaker labels, the call setup including the account name and the must-learns, the lines of the cards an Another angle press was asked on, the knowledge sections HELP used (only those still approved and unchanged), the earlier-calls items that press showed, the card HELP gave and his rating and note (kept up to date when he changes them in the review). Kept until he deletes the files; deleting a call doesn't delete moments saved from it.
- **No audio is ever saved.**
- "Export HELP feedback" (Diagnostics) writes one Markdown file to Keith's Downloads folder, only when he clicks it: the HELP lines he saw on his calls in the chosen period, the account names, his ratings, which lines he used and his notes. Keith decides where it goes from there.
- "Save support files" copies only the logs, per-call diagnostics and counters, scorecards, speed-test reports and the non-secret settings into a new folder; never the database, transcripts, call setup, keys, knowledge files, practice moments, speed-test reports that replayed them (`reports/mine`) or feedback exports.
- Saved calls are kept until Keith deletes them. An optional setting deletes calls older than 7, 14, 30 or 90 days (off by default; the first time it would delete anything, it lists the calls and asks). Deleting removes a call's transcript, speaker labels, HELP requests and cards, feedback, notes, wrap-up and email draft, and compacts the database. Numbers-only scorecards are kept.

**On screen:** the app window is hidden from screen sharing, recordings and screenshots by default (Windows 10 version 2004 and later). Keith can turn this off in Diagnostics to take a screenshot.

**Not sent anywhere else:** no analytics, no telemetry to the app's developer, nothing to GitHub. The app's source code contains no call data, keys or internal Arize documents.
